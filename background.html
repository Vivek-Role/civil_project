Background

Conventional portland cement concrete serves as the foundation of modern civil infrastructure due to its high compressive capacity, thermal stability, versatility in casting, and economic feasibility. In structural applications designed under current limit state principles (IS 456:2000) , concrete is heavily relied upon to resist compressive stresses across structural elements ranging from standard multi-story frameworks to high-performance infrastructure Neville, 2011.





Then What's the issue ???

Despite these established advantages, hardened concrete exhibits fundamental material deficiencies inherent to its quasi-brittle nature. The tensile strength of plain concrete is generally limited to roughly 10% of its compressive capacity, accompanied by a markedly low tensile strain tolerance. Under internal volumetric changes—such as plastic and drying shrinkage or hydration-induced thermal gradients—as well as sustained structural loading, micro-fissures spontaneously initiate at the microstructural level (Mehta & Monteiro, 2014).

Unchecked Crack Coalescence

As tensile stress concentrates, internal micro-cracks propagate unchecked into macro-cracks, causing rapid cleavage and catastrophic brittle failure with virtually zero post-peak energy dissipation (Shah et al., 1995).

Limitation of Rebar Reinforcement

While continuous steel rebars carry member-scale tensile loads, they cannot arrest micro-cracking phenomena originating within the matrix volume itself before macro-crack localization occurs (Mindess et al., 2003).

SOLUTION !

Addressing these inherent mechanical deficiencies requires a targeted material-level modification capable of arresting crack propagation at the microscopic scale, redistributing internal tensile stresses across opening fissures, and transforming brittle matrix failure into a ductile, energy-absorbing post-cracking response.
